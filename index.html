What is it

What Polygon staking is

Polygon staking means delegating POL to a validator that helps secure the Polygon PoS chain. You keep ownership of your POL; the validator runs the infrastructure and signs blocks and checkpoints, and you receive a share of the rewards it earns, minus its commission.

The staking token was originally MATIC. It was migrated and renamed to POL, so today you stake POL.

Where it happens

The native Polygon PoS staking contracts live on Ethereum mainnet, not on Polygon itself. Delegating, claiming and unstaking are therefore Ethereum transactions, and gas is paid in ETH.

What you choose

You pick the validator you delegate to. Validators differ in uptime, commission and how much they stake themselves, and those differences affect what you actually receive.

Native vs liquid

Native delegation vs liquid staking

There are two common ways to stake POL. Native delegation goes straight to a validator through the Polygon staking contracts. Liquid staking uses a separate protocol that issues a receipt token in exchange for your stake.

Native delegation vs liquid staking
Native delegationLiquid staking
Custody of stakeDelegated to a validator through the Polygon staking contracts; you hold the claimDeposited into a third-party protocol that manages validators for you
Liquidity during unbondingNone; funds wait out the unbonding periodA receipt token can often be traded or used elsewhere while staked
RewardsAccrue to your delegation, minus validator commission; claim or restakeUsually reflected in the receipt token, minus the protocol's fees
Extra smart-contract riskOnly the Polygon staking contracts and your validator choiceAdds the liquid-staking contracts and the receipt token's market price

Which one fits

Native delegation is simpler and has fewer moving parts. Liquid staking trades that simplicity for flexibility. This interface handles native delegation only.

How to stake

How to stake POL

Before you start

You need POL on Ethereum mainnet in your wallet, plus a little ETH for gas. If your POL is on Polygon PoS, bridge it to Ethereum first.

Step by step

  1. Connect your wallet and choose a validator, comparing uptime, commission and self-stake.
  2. Enter the amount of POL to delegate and review the estimated gas.
  3. Approve POL for the staking contract if this is your first delegation from this wallet.
  4. Sign the delegation transaction in your wallet and wait for it to confirm on Ethereum.
  5. Later, claim rewards, restake them, or start unstaking from the same wallet.

Unstaking

Unstaking starts an unbonding period of about 80 checkpoints, which is roughly a few days. Rewards do not accrue on funds that are unbonding. When the period ends, you sign a second transaction to withdraw your POL.

Fees & gas

What Polygon staking costs

Gas in ETH

Delegating, approving, claiming, restaking and withdrawing are all Ethereum transactions paid in ETH. See how gas works. Gas varies with network demand, so small stakes can be affected more by repeated claims.

Validator commission

Each validator keeps a commission from the rewards earned by its delegators. The rate is set by the validator, so compare it before you delegate. A low commission does not help if the validator has poor uptime.

Rewards are not guaranteed

Rewards accrue over checkpoints and depend on the validator's performance and network conditions. They can change over time, so treat any figure you see elsewhere as a snapshot, not a promise.

Security

Staying safe

Non-custodial by design

You connect your own wallet and sign every transaction yourself. This interface prepares the delegation, claim and unstake calls. It never holds your POL or your keys.

Before you sign

  • Check that the transaction targets the Polygon staking contracts and the validator you intended.
  • Approve only the POL amount you plan to stake, and review old token allowances now and then.
  • Look at validator uptime, commission and self-stake rather than choosing on one number.
  • Remember funds are locked during unbonding, so do not stake money you may need within days.
  • Smart contracts and validators carry risk, so it is never zero.
FAQ

Polygon Staking FAQ

What is Polygon staking?

Polygon staking means delegating POL to a validator on the Polygon PoS chain. The validator helps secure the network, and you receive a share of its rewards minus its commission. The delegation itself is recorded on Ethereum mainnet.

Do I stake MATIC or POL?

You stake POL. MATIC was migrated and renamed to POL, so new delegations use POL. If you still hold old MATIC, convert it through the official migration route before staking.

Why do I pay gas in ETH to stake on Polygon?

The native Polygon PoS staking contracts are deployed on Ethereum mainnet. Delegating, claiming and unstaking are therefore Ethereum transactions, and their gas is paid in ETH even though the token you stake is POL.

How do I choose a Polygon validator?

Compare uptime, commission and how much the validator stakes itself. A low commission is less useful if the validator misses checkpoints, so look at all three together rather than one number.

How long does unstaking POL take?

Unstaking starts an unbonding period of about 80 checkpoints, which is roughly a few days. The exact time depends on how quickly checkpoints are submitted. When it ends, you sign a withdrawal transaction to receive your POL.

Can I claim or restake Polygon staking rewards?

Yes. Rewards accrue to your delegation over checkpoints, and you can either claim them to your wallet or restake them into the same validator. Each action is an Ethereum transaction that costs gas.
